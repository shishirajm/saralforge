When an enquiry becomes a booking, work passes through several hands: yours, your staff's, your customer's and perhaps a tool's. Jobs are often lost at the handoffs, when a message sits unanswered, a quote is never followed up, or two people book the same slot. Mapping the handoffs shows where to improve.

Draw the path you actually follow

On one page, write each step from first contact to confirmed booking. For each step, add three things: who owns it, what they need to proceed, and what happens if it does not happen. Do this for the path as it is today, not as it should be.

Name the statuses

A short list of statuses that everyone uses prevents most confusion. For example: New, Qualified, Quoted, Confirmed, Done and Lost. Every enquiry should always be in exactly one of them. "Lost" is useful: it lets you notice why work is not converting.

  • Every enquiry has one status at all times.
  • One named person owns each status and moves the enquiry on.
  • An enquiry cannot stay in New or Quoted without someone being reminded.

Where humans stay in charge

Some steps benefit from a person's judgement: whether to take an unusual job, what to quote, how to handle an upset customer. Tools can prepare, remind and record. Keep a person responsible for decisions that affect money, safety or reputation.

Hypothetical example

This is an invented path for a fictional wedding photographer. It is not a Saral Forge client result.

  1. An enquiry arrives by form or message and is recorded in one list as New, with a note of the channel.
  2. The photographer replies the same day with a few questions about date, place and style. The status becomes Qualified.
  3. She checks her calendar. If the date is taken, she tells the couple at once. Failure path: the date is unavailable, so she offers an alternative and marks the enquiry Lost if they decline.
  4. She sends a quote and a link to confirm. The status becomes Quoted, with a reminder in five days.
  5. The couple accept and pay a deposit. She marks it Confirmed and adds the date to her calendar. Failure path: the deposit does not arrive within a set time, so she follows up before releasing the date.
  6. If the date changes, she updates the calendar and the record the same day.
  7. After the wedding, she sends a thank-you and asks for feedback.

What to fix first

  1. Collect enquiries in one place. This alone prevents many losses.
  2. Reply faster. Even a short acknowledgement tells the customer you have it.
  3. Add chase dates to quotes.
  4. Pick one calendar as the source of truth for availability.
  5. Only then consider tools to automate parts of it.

When your booking rules are unusual

Ask for help when the booking rules are specific to your business, when several people book against the same limited resources, or when enquiries come through several channels that you want to bring together.
