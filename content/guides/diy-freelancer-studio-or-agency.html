There is no single best way to get a website or app built. Each option suits some projects and fails others. The useful question is which one fits yours, given its complexity, your time, and how long you will need it to keep working.

Saral Forge offers these services, so it has an interest in this question. Treat this guide as a checklist for any provider, including us, and make up your own mind.

The four options in plain terms

  • Do it yourself. You use a builder, existing tools or AI assistance, and you do the work.
  • A freelancer. One independent person, who may cover design, building or both.
  • A studio. A business that offers several skills under one name, usually with a closer relationship than a large agency.
  • An agency. A larger organisation with specialist roles such as project management, design, development and testing.

These are tendencies, not rules. A skilled freelancer can outperform a poor agency, and a small studio is not automatically better than either.

Questions to ask any provider

  • Can you show work like mine, and tell me what you did on it? Look for real, verifiable examples, and be wary of unnamed claims. Ask each provider, Saral Forge included, which examples are real projects and which are concepts.
  • What questions do you have about my project? Good providers ask a lot.
  • What is included in the quote, and what would cost extra?
  • Who exactly will do the work, and who can I speak to if there is a problem?
  • How will we handle changes once work has started?
  • What do I own at the end, and how will it be handed over? See the ownership checklist.
  • Who looks after it after launch, and how is that arranged?
  • What happens if you are unavailable?

Questions to ask yourself

  • How much of my own time can I give this each week?
  • How clear is the problem? A vague problem is risky with any provider, so write a brief first.
  • How costly would a mistake be, such as lost data or a failed launch?
  • How long will I need this to keep working, and who will look after it?
  • Do I need one skill or several, such as design, building, security and content?

A simple way to compare

  1. Send the same brief to two or three providers of different kinds.
  2. Compare their questions as well as their prices.
  3. Ask each how they would reduce the scope. The answers show how they think.
  4. Check references or examples that you can verify.
  5. Choose the one whose plan you understand best, not the one with the most impressive pitch.

Hypothetical example

A fictional florist wants a five-page website with a gallery. A freelancer or a builder would likely suit her, and she has the time to supply photos and text. A fictional logistics company wants a system linking quotes, scheduling and invoicing with staff logins. That has several moving parts and long-term upkeep, so a studio or agency with a broader range of skills, and a clear plan for maintenance, may suit it better.
