Typing the same customer details into your enquiry inbox, your calendar and your accounting software is not just slow. Each copy is a chance for a typo, and over time the copies stop agreeing. The fix is rarely "connect everything". It is to decide where each fact lives and move it one way.

Step one: map where information starts

Before choosing any tool, write down what actually happens to a piece of information, such as a new enquiry.

  1. List every place the information is typed or pasted, in order.
  2. Note who does it and how often it happens.
  3. Note which copies are used for decisions, and which are just kept "in case".
  4. Mark the first place the information appears. This is where it starts.

This map often shows that two of the five places could simply be dropped.

Step two: choose the authoritative record

The authoritative record is the one place you trust if two copies disagree. Every important fact should have exactly one. Other systems may read it, but should not edit it.

When a simple import is enough

  • The information changes slowly, for example once a day or less.
  • Nobody is harmed if the copy is a few hours behind.
  • One person can check the result in a few minutes.
  • Both systems can export or import a file with the fields you need.

If most of these are true, a scheduled import is often better than a live integration because it is easier to understand and to fix.

A system-connection checklist

Before connecting two systems, find out:

  • Does each product offer a supported way to read and write data, or only to export?
  • Who holds the admin access needed to set it up, and where will the access keys be stored safely?
  • What identifies a record uniquely, such as an email address or an ID, so that duplicates can be detected?
  • What happens when one system is unavailable? Will the update be retried or lost?
  • Who is told when something fails, and how?
  • What are the exceptions: cancelled jobs, changed details, customers with two addresses?
  • Can you test with a copy of real, messy data rather than clean examples?
  • How would you undo a wrong update?

An illustrative enquiry-to-job flow

Hypothetical example

This is an invented flow for a fictional small plumbing business. It is not a client result.

  1. An enquiry arrives through the website form and is added to one shared list, the authoritative record.
  2. A person reviews it, checks it is in the service area and marks it Quoting or Declined.
  3. When marked Quoting, a quote record is created once, using the customer's details from the list.
  4. When the customer approves, the job appears in the schedule. The calendar reads it and does not own it.
  5. When the job is complete, the invoice details move to accounting once.
  6. If a step fails, for example the schedule cannot be updated, the person responsible is told and the record stays flagged until it is fixed.

The important decisions are the single list, the one-way movement and what happens on failure. The tool you pick matters less.

When the connection is no longer a DIY job

Ask for help when records must stay in step in real time, when a mistake costs money, or when your systems offer no official way to connect.
