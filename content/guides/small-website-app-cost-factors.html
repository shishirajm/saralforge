This is not a price list. Prices depend on your project, your provider and the market, and any figure published without those details would mislead you. What this guide offers instead is an explanation of what makes a quote go up or down, so you can compare quotes sensibly.

Why quotes for the "same" project differ

Two providers can quote very different amounts because they are not quoting the same thing. They may assume different numbers of pages or screens, different amounts of testing, different levels of support, or different things you will supply yourself. A low quote may leave out work that a high quote includes. Compare what is covered before you compare totals.

Fixed price or time-based

A fixed price gives certainty about the total when the scope is clearly defined, but changes will be quoted separately. Time-based pricing, where you pay for the time spent, suits work where the scope will change, but you need good reporting and a budget limit. Neither is better in general. A vague scope is risky under both, which is why scoping a first version comes first.

A total-cost checklist

The build is only part of what you will spend. Include each of these when you compare options, including the "free" or cheap ones.

  • The build or setup.
  • Your own time, for supplying content, testing and decisions.
  • Domain name and hosting, each year.
  • Subscriptions and licences for tools the site or app depends on.
  • Maintenance, updates and support after launch.
  • Changes you will want within the first year.
  • Training for the people who will use it.
  • The cost of leaving later, such as exporting data or moving to another platform.

Hypothetical example

A fictional café owner receives two quotes for a website with online ordering. The first is cheaper but does not mention testing, content or hosting. The second costs more and states all three, as well as who looks after the site. Using the worksheet, she sees that the first quote leaves out several things she would otherwise pay for separately, or that would simply not be done. The totals were not comparable until she wrote down what each included.

Before you ask for quotes

  • Write a brief so every provider quotes the same thing.
  • List what you will supply yourself, and be honest about when.
  • Ask each provider the same questions from the table.
  • Decide in advance what the first version leaves out.

When quotes differ and you cannot see why

Ask for advice when quotes differ widely and you cannot see why, when the project involves other systems or sensitive data, or when you are not sure what to include in a first version.
